# Cush Score policy

## Rules

- Each assessment covers one account holder only. Never use dependants, household size, household income, or household affordability adjustments.
- The prompt's component weights are available funds 35%, income strength 30%, cash-flow behaviour 20%, and credit history 15%.
- The score is 0–1000. Confidence is separate, 1–100 when an assessment exists; it describes evidence quality and completeness, not financial strength.
- Confidence rules: history earns 5/month up to 6 months (30); source quality is bank connection 25 or verified statement 15 weighted by data share; credit check is matched 15, thin 10, no match 5, not done 0; freshness is 10/6/3 within 7/30/90 days; account coverage is up to 10 reduced by unresolved-transfer share; income source is UK payslip/credits 10, steady employer 8, variable 5, UK offer 4, family support only 3. Each confirmed integrity flag deducts 5; floor at 1. Bands are 1–39 Low, 40–69 Medium, 70–100 High.
- Do not cap confidence at 80 for family-support-only income unless the user changes this decision.
- Thin credit file maps to neutral 50 only when Interswitch explicitly confirms thin-file status. Approved Interswitch repayment mapping: settled=1, overdue=0.5, confirmed missed/default=0; pending/unknown are excluded, and conflicting reports remain unscored.
- An Interswitch credit-history lookup is required for a complete Cush Score. BVN submission requires separate explicit consent to Interswitch; never persist the BVN or raw provider response. Keep only the latest normalized evidence counts.
- Start with a flat £1,000/month baseline for all UK users; the amount, effective date, and rationale belong in a reviewed, versioned policy, and scoring must never fall back to a hardcoded value when no active policy exists.
- V2 scoring is GBP-normalized. Reviewed Mono statement data may originate in GBP, USD, or NGN; use the user's approved CBN central rates by transaction date for USD/NGN conversion.
- Count accessible funds, avoid double counting, and exclude one-off income from recurring income. Self-declared financial amounts may contribute to provisional component scores but remain self-reported; family support may count as the applicant's own inflow when regular and supported.
- Use 5 source-quality points for self-declared records only after the authorized admin activates a policy version containing that value. Self-declared entries must not satisfy verified-data completion gates or be represented as independently verified.
- Show a provisional overall score as supported evidence becomes scoreable, normalize available weights to 0–1000, and show progress separately. Keep component-level values and scoring factors out of customer-facing pages and APIs; reserve them for admin review.
- Financial history counts by supported statement month within the assessment window, not by import timestamp. A genuine, identity-verified credit report remains score-eligible regardless of age; freshness may affect confidence, not score eligibility.
- Assess source dates automatically; use retrieval dates as a confidence fallback and route uncertain-date cases to admin review, without age alone disqualifying valid evidence.
- Mono statement data may feed V2 only after user review and consent. The user selected Interswitch's credit-history lookup and approved storing only normalized derived counts; approved repayment mapping is versioned separately from the active destination/confidence policy.

**Why:** The user supplied the scoring prompt and explicitly approved replacing the legacy 40/30/30 calculation with V2. They later changed the earlier all-or-nothing score rule: users should see a normalized, provisional score as they complete criteria, with coverage shown separately. On 2026-11-01 they replaced the prior location-based baselines with a flat £1,000 UK baseline and said component scores are for admins, not users. On 2026-11-02 they clarified that the baseline must be editable within a reviewed, versioned policy and that per-user component details are an admin-only view. They also clarified that old evidence remains eligible when its underlying period is relevant, that an authentic credit report must not be excluded solely for age, and that source dates should be checked automatically with admin fallback for uncertain dates. They approved CBN central rates with transaction-date conversion for USD/NGN data, supplied the six-factor confidence rules, chose no family-support-only cap for now, and require Interswitch credit history for a final score. They approved separate BVN consent for Interswitch, minimal normalized-data retention, and the settled/overdue/missed-default formula. In the later evidence-intake guidance, the user changed the no-manual-scoring rule: manual entries should affect the assessment immediately as self-declared, and chose 5 Confidence source-quality points. Freshness must reflect the reported source period or source refresh, not merely resaving a form.

**How to apply:** Normalize V2 calculations against the approved £1,000/month UK baseline. Supported evidence alone determines whether the final score is complete; manual entries may contribute to provisional funds, income, and complete monthly cash-flow components, but never satisfy verification or completion requirements. Use CBN month-specific rates only for supported GBP/USD/NGN conversion; fail explicitly if a recent rate is unavailable. Keep uploaded-document extraction and review separate from scoring unless a versioned scoring policy and evidence pipeline explicitly approve that use. Keep Confidence separate, preserve current component weights and the 0–1000 scale, and never fabricate missing baselines, provider evidence, or exchange rates. Manual source-quality credit is 5 only under an activated policy version. Never submit a BVN without separate explicit consent for Interswitch; do not store BVNs or raw provider reports. Apply provider repayment mappings only to verified normalized evidence, and award thin-file 50 only when Interswitch explicitly confirms it.
